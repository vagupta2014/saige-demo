Specialty Property Underwriting — Product Concept
A product layer for LQM-powered catastrophe risk assessment at the individual policy level. Built to test a specific hypothesis about where the bottleneck actually lives.
Persona 1 · Actuarial Team
Catastrophe Analyst
Owns portfolio-level cat model runs. Sets pricing guidelines and appetite limits that underwriters work from.
Runs RMS / Verisk AIR on full portfolio — hours to days per run
Produces quarterly EP curves, AAL, PML for board and reinsurance
Handles per-submission model run requests from underwriting
Problem 1 — Compute speed
Portfolio runs quarterly. Per-submission model run: 2–5 business day turnaround on request.
SAIGE LQM solves this ↗
Milliseconds per run. Same computation, no batch cycle required.
Persona 2 · Underwriting Team ★ This prototype's user
Alison Chen
Property Underwriting Analyst · Makes bind / decline / condition decisions on individual high-value submissions. Authority limit ≤$5M replacement value.
Evaluates high-value properties ($1M–$10M replacement cost)
Works from actuarial guidelines and pre-run cat outputs — not live models
At bind time: no live portfolio accumulation context — works from quarterly snapshot
Problem 2 — Workflow gap
Analyst works from stale quarterly data. Per-submission model run requires 2–5 day actuarial request. No live view at bind time.
This prototype tests whether ↗
A purpose-designed product layer closes this gap — real-time LQM output directly at the analyst's decision point, with live portfolio context and a governance artifact.
What "policy" means in this prototype
A single insurance contract on a specialty high-value residential property in a catastrophe-exposed zone — the type written by Chubb, AIG Private Client, Travelers High Value, or Lloyd's syndicates. Replacement value $1M–$10M. Individual cat model assessment required before bind. Not standard mass-market (State Farm, Progressive) where policies are batch-priced from rate tables without per-property cat model runs.
Hypothesis being tested

If you put LQM output directly in the underwriting analyst's hands — with live portfolio context, interactive term controls, and a governance artifact — she can make a defensible bind decision in under 5 minutes without routing through the cat modeling team. Portfolio accumulation updates at bind time, not quarterly. Pattern: Upstart Auto (UAF) went from 5% → 9.7% bind conversion by giving Finance Managers levers, not black boxes. Same ML. Better interface.

⚡ Product Principle · from Upstart Auto
At Upstart Auto, the Credit First feature came from 8 weeks shadowing Finance Managers in dealerships — not interviews, but live workflow observation. We didn't design until we could name exactly where the old process failed. That map became the product brief. Applied here: every SAIGE screen targets a specific gap in the legacy cat model workflow — the 2–5 day actuarial request, the quarterly portfolio PDF, the manual audit trail. These gaps are observed from secondary research and industry signals; the hypothesis is to be validated with direct underwriter research. Designing from observed workflow failure — not assumed need — is what produces adoption rather than features.

⚡ Product Principle · from Upstart Auto
At Upstart Auto, Finance Managers asked "why should I trust this AI approval over my instinct?" The answer wasn't explaining the algorithm — it was Real-Time Alternatives: Upstart's offer next to the traditional deal, side by side in the same screen. Counterintuitively, inviting the comparison built more trust than any product claim. Applied here: if SAIGE LQM is genuinely more accurate than RMS and Verisk, a transparent back-test on real storms proves it. An underwriter who can't see that comparison will check elsewhere anyway — and likely revert to the vendor she already trusts.

⚡ Product Principle · from Upstart Auto
At Upstart Auto, Finance Managers chose which offer to present — their income depended on closing the right deal. They needed to trust an AI output enough to stake their customer relationship on it. The fix wasn't explaining the model — it was the Rehashing screen: change a term, see the updated outcome instantly, no submit. Adoption followed once the analyst had control. Applied here: Alison is making the same kind of judgment call — bind or decline, within risk appetite, to maximize portfolio return. SAIGE provides property-level loss precision legacy models can't match. Her market knowledge plus LQM accuracy is what makes each bind both defensible and higher ROI than the legacy workflow.
Hypothesis test: if the analyst finds viable terms here without routing to the cat team, the workflow gap from the cover is closed.
On the 5 levers: these are underwriter-controllable policy terms — not raw risk inputs. The underlying property risk (location, construction, coastal proximity) is already captured in SAIGE's AAL. Alison is deciding how much of that risk the carrier accepts and at what terms. Which levers belong here is a hypothesis to validate with underwriters.

🎬 Screen 4 — What's happening
Alison reviews the complete deal record. She clicks Generate Model Report PDF to produce the compliance artifact, then Bind Policy →. Portfolio accumulation updates automatically at bind.
Legacy workflow
Governance docs created manually post-bind — often missing model version, confidence bands, or scenario context.
With SAIGE · compliance unlocked
Florida OIR and Solvency II–formatted record auto-generated. Chief Actuary can adopt without a separate regulatory submission.
⚡ Product Principle · from Upstart Auto
At Upstart Auto, automating document verification required the product to generate the compliance artifact — not leave documentation to the loan officer after the fact. An ML system with no audit trail is undeployable in a regulated environment regardless of its accuracy. Applied here: a Chief Actuary won't approve SAIGE for production use without an auto-generated, model-versioned, regulatory-formatted record at every bind. This screen isn't the last step — it's what makes every prior screen trustworthy enough to adopt at scale.
Reg context: Florida OIR: cat model certification required for rate-making use. Solvency II Art. 101/120: SCR at 99.5% VaR · use test requires model integration into underwriting decisions. NAIC ORSA: annual self-assessment for carriers >$500M written premium.
